# Coolant temperature, thermostat and overheating
tags: coolant, thermostat, P0128, P0217, overheating, warm-up, fan, water pump

A healthy petrol engine reaches 85-95 °C coolant temperature within about 10-15 minutes of driving (longer for diesels in winter) and then stays stable. Watching the warm-up curve across sessions is one of the best early warnings available from OBD data:

- A slower warm-up than before, or a temperature that drops when cruising, means the thermostat is sticking open (P0128 follows). It causes poor heating and fuel economy, and the catalyst/O2 monitors may not run.
- A temperature above about 105-110 °C, or one that climbs in traffic but recovers when moving, points to a cooling fan or fan relay fault, low coolant, or a clogged radiator.
- Rapid overheating, coolant loss with no visible leak, white sweet-smelling exhaust, or bubbles in the expansion tank point to a possible head gasket failure.

Never open a hot cooling system. If the temperature gauge reaches the red zone, stop the engine safely; continuing to drive can warp the cylinder head within minutes.
