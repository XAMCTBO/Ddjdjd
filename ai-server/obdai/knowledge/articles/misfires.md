# Diagnosing misfires (P0300-P0308)
tags: misfire, P0300, P0301, P0302, P0303, P0304, coil, spark plug, injector, compression

P030X names the cylinder (P0301 = cylinder 1); P0300 is random/multiple cylinders.

Single cylinder procedure (cheapest first):
1. Swap the ignition coil of the misfiring cylinder with a neighbouring cylinder, clear codes, and drive. If the misfire moves to the other cylinder, the coil is bad.
2. If it doesn't move, inspect or swap the spark plug: look for cracks, oil fouling, or a worn gap.
3. If it still stays, suspect the injector (swap, or check resistance and listen for clicking) and then do a compression or leak-down test.

Random misfire (P0300) usually has a shared cause: worn plugs on all cylinders, a vacuum leak (check fuel trims), low fuel pressure, poor fuel, or EGR stuck open. A flashing check-engine light means an active severe misfire: reduce load and stop driving soon, because it can overheat and destroy the catalytic converter within minutes.

Damp coil wells (from washing or a leaking cowl) cause misfires on startup in wet weather; oil in plug wells means valve cover or tube seals are leaking.
