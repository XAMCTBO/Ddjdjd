# Turbo boost and diesel aftertreatment (DPF, EGR, SCR)
tags: turbo, boost, P0299, P0234, DPF, P2463, P242F, EGR, SCR, AdBlue, diesel, regeneration

Underboost (P0299) is most often a boost leak: a split intercooler hose or a loose clamp. Then check a sticking wastegate or variable vanes, the boost control solenoid, and its vacuum lines. A pressure test of the charge-air system finds leaks fast.

A diesel particulate filter (DPF) traps soot and burns it during regeneration, which needs about 15-20 minutes of steady driving above roughly 60 km/h. Repeated short trips stop regeneration and lead to soot-loading codes (P2463). Ash (P242F) can't be burnt off and needs cleaning or replacement after high mileage. Faulty EGR valves, injectors, or turbo oil leaks increase soot production.

SCR/AdBlue faults (P20EE, and P20xx in general) are often caused by poor or crystallised DEF fluid, a clogged DEF injector, or a NOx sensor. Many cars limit power or restart counts when SCR faults persist.
