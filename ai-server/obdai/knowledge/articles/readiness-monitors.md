# Readiness monitors and drive cycles
tags: readiness, monitors, inspection, emissions test, drive cycle, clear codes, Mode 04

Readiness monitors are self-tests the ECU runs on emission systems: misfire, fuel system, and components (continuous), plus catalyst, EVAP, O2 sensor, O2 heater, EGR, and secondary air on petrol engines, or NMHC catalyst, NOx/SCR, boost, exhaust gas sensor, and PM filter on diesels.

Clearing codes (Mode 04) or disconnecting the battery resets every monitor to "incomplete". Most inspection stations reject a car with incomplete monitors: US states typically allow at most one incomplete monitor for 2001+ vehicles, and two for 1996-2000.

A generic drive cycle that completes most monitors:
1. Cold start: let the car sit for 8+ hours, with fuel between 1/4 and 3/4.
2. Idle for 2-3 minutes with the headlights and rear defroster on.
3. Accelerate gently to 90 km/h and cruise steadily for 3-5 minutes.
4. Coast down without braking to about 30 km/h.
5. Mix city driving with steady cruising for 20-30 minutes in total.

Some monitors (EVAP especially) can need several days of normal driving. A used car with freshly cleared codes and incomplete monitors is a warning sign: the seller may be hiding a fault.
