# Oxygen sensors: upstream vs downstream
tags: O2 sensor, lambda, P0130, P0133, P0135, P0136, P0141, wideband, narrowband

Upstream (sensor 1, before the catalyst) controls fuelling. A narrowband sensor switches between about 0.1 V (lean) and 0.9 V (rich); when warm at 2500 rpm a healthy one crosses 0.45 V at least 5-8 times every 10 seconds. A slowing switch rate over months means the sensor is ageing (P0133 follows). Wideband/air-fuel sensors report lambda (≈1.00 at stoichiometric) instead of switching.

Downstream (sensor 2, after the catalyst) monitors the catalyst and should be steady at about 0.6-0.75 V.

Heater circuit codes (P0135, P0141, P0030, P0050) are usually the sensor's internal heater: check the fuse first, then the heater resistance. Sensors typically last 100 000-160 000 km. Contamination from coolant (head gasket), oil burning, or silicone sealants shortens their life - replace the cause, not just the sensor.
