# EVAP leaks (P0440, P0442, P0455, P0456)
tags: EVAP, P0440, P0442, P0455, P0456, fuel cap, purge valve, vent valve, smoke test

The evaporative emissions system traps fuel vapour in a charcoal canister and burns it via the purge valve. The ECU checks for leaks by sealing the system (vent valve closed) and watching tank pressure or vacuum.

- P0455 (large leak): most often a loose or missing fuel cap, or a disconnected hose.
- P0442 / P0456 (small / very small leak): worn cap seal, cracked hose, a purge or vent valve not sealing, or a rusty filler neck.

Order of work: tighten or replace the fuel cap (cheapest), clear codes, and complete a drive cycle. Then visually check the hoses, test the purge valve (it should hold vacuum when unpowered), and finally do a smoke test.

The EVAP monitor only runs under specific conditions: fuel level typically between 15 % and 85 %, a cold start where coolant and ambient temperatures are close, and ambient temperature in a moderate band (usually about 4-35 °C). In freezing weather the monitor often won't run at all, so a repair can't be confirmed until temperatures rise. This is normal.
